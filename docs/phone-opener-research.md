# Phone page opener research

- Checked: 7 November 2026. Twenty live homepages, read directly and checked visually.
- Phone counts: approximate visible words in a fresh, unscrolled 390 × 844 phone viewport, light mode, after loading. Includes headings, navigation, dates, visible form text and image lettering. Excludes hidden text and content below the fold. Counts describe the whole screen, not just the introduction.
- Visual evidence: headless Chrome phone emulation only, not Vansh's Chrome or a physical phone. Browser bars are excluded. Patrick Collison and Paul Graham retain a desktop layout scaled down on mobile.
- Quotes: opening excerpts, at most 15 words per site. Where there is no introductory sentence, the opening heading, menu or article is identified.
- Selection: fifteen sites from the supplied shortlist, plus Emil Kowalski, Dan Abramov, Paul Graham, Tania Rascia and Simon Willison. Recognition for writing or design does not make every opening a good fit for this brief.

## 2. Patterns

- **Strong for this brief:** Say what the person makes or works on immediately. [Brian](https://brianlovin.com/) names role, place and employer in one sentence; [Maggie](https://maggieappleton.com/) names her output; [Emil](https://emilkowal.ski/) gives an honest current status. Specific work carries more information than a broad mission.
- **Weaker for this brief:** [Nat](https://nat.org/) and [Tania](https://www.taniarascia.com/) put childhood before current projects; [Kent](https://kentcdodds.com/) offers a general mission; [Patrick](https://patrickcollison.com/) requires a click for context. [Linus](https://thesephist.com/) leads with research but spends much of the screen explaining it. These serve other purposes; they are weaker models for this opener.
- **The past:** [Paco](https://paco.me/) and Emil compress previous work into one sentence. [Derek](https://sive.rs/) uses short career eras and links to longer About and Now pages. Brian omits career history from his intro. For Vansh: one present-day paragraph first; the longer story can follow.

## 3. Three openers for Vansh

- Facts and voice: [lead.now in story.ts](../src/data/story.ts), [profile.ts](../src/data/profile.ts), and [Intro in content-draft.md](content-draft.md#intro). The eight-person team comes from the Intro; hiring comes from lead.now.
- Current order: [GraphicWalk.astro](../src/components/GraphicWalk.astro) prints the name and role, then [story-path.ts](../src/lib/phone/story-path.ts) begins in Delhi; lead.now appears in the final Floqer stop.

### Option 1: Role, place, work

- **Opener, 29 words:** I'm Head of Engineering at Floqer. We work out of a hacker house in downtown Toronto, building agents that research companies and people so sales teams don't have to.
- **Pattern:** Brian Lovin's compact role-and-place introduction, with the actual work named before any history.

### Option 2: Work, role, people

- **Opener, 33 words:** I build agents that research companies and people so sales teams don't have to. I'm Head of Engineering at Floqer, where eight of us work out of a hacker house in downtown Toronto.
- **Pattern:** Maggie Appleton's output-first opening, followed by role and a concrete detail about the team.

### Option 3: Work and hiring now

- **Opener, 33 words:** I'm Head of Engineering at Floqer, building agents that research companies and people so sales teams don't have to. I'm also hiring for the hacker house we work out of in downtown Toronto.
- **Pattern:** Emil Kowalski's explicit current-status opening, adapted to two present activities: building and hiring.
