# 55. The content page: the whole story as text, on /content

2026-10-25. His words: the journey's content "shouldn't be a scroll kind of thing. The content should show up when we are entering the room. It just shows up there properly. It's not patchy scrolls." Before that, "let's only build a content-only page first", "a whole text about me", "designed: no graphics or anything".

## What was built

- `src/data/story.ts`: the story as data. A lead (role, his LinkedIn line, the summary) and twelve chapters, one per set of the journey in the order it walks them, each with year, span, place, title, short paragraphs and a mono line of figures. The words are the draft's (`docs/content-draft.md`), his facts only. Chapters 2010 and 2013 are marked `his`: placeholders until he writes them.
- `src/pages/content.astro`: the page. First cut was a timeline (year gutter, place label, title, figures line, now rows). He saw it in his Chrome: "Not a timeline", the wordmark's hover rewrapped the name, "just simple text, different paragraphs", "just fonts and colors". Second cut: the name, the role line, then paragraphs only, a wider gap between chapters, a line of links at the end. Years folded into the first sentence where the gutter had carried them. Year, place, title and figures stay in the data for the rooms.
- `Base.astro` gained `noindex`; `/content` uses it.

## The text, and the phone

Later the same night: "I'm going to explain things to you... it has to be similar to how I write my LinkedIn post". Read in his Chrome: his posts back to Shivam's team post (SF Tech Week, disposable software, Disrupt Top 20, the hiring post, the graduation post). The voice: first person, short plain sentences, a concrete detail over a claim, a small aside at the end, no sales words. The first paragraph is the old site's "I'm a 3rd year... I barely manage to attend classes" slot rewritten for now; the journey begins where the old site began it, "The journey began when I won Google Code-in at 17", and runs to Floqer. Ten chapters in `story.ts`; the same text in `docs/content-draft.md`.

"What we are building is going to be a phone view... an amazing animation... bring me a lot of variations." Six quick CSS scroll driven variants (rail, sticky years, rooms, swipe, reading light, watermark) in a 390 by 844 frame: "None of this is good." He stepped back: the rooms concept, "a bit dark in the background", "an animation just moving around"; 3D on a phone only if 100% sure, and the page must work on old phones and in the Instagram and LinkedIn in-app browsers. The real 3D stage does render at its phone tier in portrait (headless, `?tier=0`), but 41 MB of assets and WebGL in a five-year-old WebView cannot be promised, so the phone journey is 2D rooms:

- First cut of the rooms: every chapter the same dark box of five CSS planes, a lamp, a door. His word: "This looks so ugly. Why is everything the same? Why can it not be like you are creating CSS in the back of my room? Then when we scroll, those pieces just move the CSS in 2D into something else, the next thing... You're not using how we would design the 3D models for the desktop, those practices."
- Second cut, kept: `src/lib/phone/scenes.ts` holds thirteen rooms as flat pictures, each the stage's own set in 2D (sets.ts read for what stands in each: the apartment's glass and desk and bed, the 2010 room's grille and Xbox and poster, the lab's bench, the Android lawn with the block and his photographs by the door, the 2020 desk under books and awards, the port window seat and the moving map, the hall's tiers and the screen, Bean's trestle table with the Opera House and the bridge in the window, the three cities of the walk in their light, Volta lit at the end of the snow, the Cohn's proscenium and banner, Floqer's brick F and T of desks and the stair to his door). One cast of nineteen pieces stands in every room (background, far, two windows, floor, rail, door, frame, shelf, lamp, bed, two desks, chair, three screens, two small things); a room gives each a place, size, colour, style and radius. `src/pages/v/rooms.astro` moves them: the first half of a room's scroll holds it and carries a long chapter's words up, the second half eases every piece into its place in the next room, so the apartment's monitors become the Delhi screen and the Xbox, the lab's bench Google's path, the hall's tiers the promenade's rail. Styles are the detail a piece wears (grille, curtain, tiers, sails, skyline, brick, the F), as background gradients and clip paths; what moves on its own is small and per room (the pendant's pulse, the screens' flicker, a seaplane, leaves, snow, the ferry).
- Then: "the animations for text are so bad. no one asked you for that. also make these things vertical not horizontal. and the text should be a simple scroll... and the text is on top of it." So: the picture is the whole portrait screen, fixed behind; the room stands in its top half (the furniture set 6 units down, 1.3 times the authored height, the wall above and the floor running to the foot); a scrim dims it a little above and nearly black below; the words are plain document flow over it, each chapter from 58% down the screen, no fades, no rides. A line 40% down the screen reads which chapter is on; the pieces move in the last stretch before the next chapter's top crosses it, so the new room is there as its words arrive.
- Then: "it's the other way around. i need the text on top left and then graphics in the back, it is still horizontal. there is a black blur." So: no scrim at all; the room stands tall, its floor line 70% down the screen, the furniture 1.3 times its authored size about the middle, the wall and ceiling above, the floor to the foot; the words top left from 9% down, in each room's own ink (`ink` in scenes.ts: paper in the dark rooms, near black in the light ones), the body's colour easing between rooms. A line 45% down reads the chapter; the pieces move in the last stretch before the next chapter's top crosses it.
- Then: "it should be one continuous text like earlier", "and much simpler". The words are one continuous text as on /content (the name, the role line, the paragraphs, 40px between chapters), the rooms changing behind as each chapter's top crosses a line 45% down. Every room cut to four to seven pieces (fourteen in the cast now: background, far, window, floor, rail, door, frame, lamp, bed, desk, three screens, one small thing), the patterns on floors, water and curtains gone, the leaves, snow, seaplane and ferry gone; the pendant's light and the screens' glow are what still moves.
- Built for old phones and in-app browsers: window scroll, one requestAnimationFrame per scroll, transform, opacity and colour only, no WebGL, no scroll-driven CSS, no `inset`, no flex `gap`, no `color-mix` in the page's own palette, `-webkit-clip-path`, geometry measured once per width, the hidden layout only where module scripts run. Without JavaScript a plain dark page with every word. Reduced motion: the pieces cut rather than move, nothing drifts.
- `/v` frames it in a 390 by 844 phone on a desktop and hands a phone straight to `/v/rooms`. Phone test on his wifi: the Mac's LAN address, port 4323 (`astro preview --host`).
- Measured headless with a 4x CPU throttle through four rooms: 281 frames, 16.7 ms average, none over 34 ms. Every room at its hold, five mid-moves, the long chapters riding up, the end: `.cache/content/sheet-m1.png`, `sheet-m2.png`, `sheet-m3.png`, `sheet-t.png`.

## The walk

"This is not it. Let's just do something like: text is up top. The bottom 40% is covered by the animation of me walking, like a Mario game or something like that, while we scroll and I'm going through different phases of life. Build that vision. Make sure it's perfect on that."

- `src/pages/v/walk.astro`: the words up top, one continuous text as on /content; the bottom two fifths a fixed side scroller. He is a 14 by 20 pixel figure (hair, skin, dark hoodie, jeans, white sneakers) drawn as one box-shadow per pixel, scaled four times: an idle frame and a four-frame walk, a frame every 22 px of ground, standing once the page stops. He stands 30% in from the left; the world slides past him in three layers at 0.35, 0.7 and 1 of his speed.
- `src/lib/phone/walk.ts`: thirteen places in the story's order, each its sky or wall, its ground and its props in the three layers, following the stage's sets: the apartment's glass and city and pendant, the Delhi grille and Xbox and poster, the lab bench with a station every metre, the sun and trees and Androids and Google's block, the 2020 desk under the books and the curtain, the aisle of the plane with a porthole and a seat back every row, the hall's screen and lectern and tiers, Bean's window with the sails and the pendants and the table, the three cities of the walk (mountains and sails, the skyline and the tower, the hill and the ferry and Volta lit at the end), the Cohn's banner and stage, Floqer's brick F and desks and the stair to his door. A door stands at the end of every place.
- The world is built from where the chapters stand on the page: he enters place k when chapter k's top is 35% down the screen. A short chapter still gets 560 px of world (he walks faster through it); each place is a clipped segment in each layer, so one place's hills never show behind the next. The strip's colour turns to the next place's over the last stretch before its door.
- Built for old phones and in-app browsers as before: window scroll, one requestAnimationFrame per scroll, transforms only, no WebGL, no scroll-driven CSS, `-webkit-clip-path`, geometry built once per width. Without JavaScript the words stand alone. Measured headless at 4x CPU throttle: no frame over 34 ms. Frames: `.cache/content/sheet-w1.png`, `sheet-w2.png`, `sheet-w3.png`.
- The rooms pages and their data are gone; `/v` frames the walk on a desktop and hands a phone to `/v/walk`.
